Housing and Wages

How have housing costs moved against Florida incomes and wages?

Who decides
Housing prices are set largely by markets. State and local governments shape them through zoning, housing programs, insurance rules and property taxes.
What these charts establish
How far housing costs and incomes have moved apart, in inflation-adjusted dollars, and which parts of a homeowner's bill rose. It does not attribute the gap to any single policy.
  • Florida's median rent rose 91% from 2010 to 2024, to $1,812 a month.
  • Florida's median home value rose 142% from 2010 to 2024, to $396,900.
  • Since 2010, Florida's median rent rose 33% and median home value 68% after inflation, against 22% for median household income.
  • 62.1% of Florida renters spent 30% or more of income on rent in 2024, the highest share since at least 2010.
  • Florida had 41 affordable and available rental units for every 100 renter households earning up to 60% of area median income in 2024.
  • At Florida's median wage, a month of median rent took 80 hours of work in 2024, against 62 nationally.
  • Florida's average pay rose 18.5% after inflation from 2010 to 2025, faster than the U.S. (14.0%), and remains 7.6% below the U.S. average.
  • Adjusted for Florida's price level, its median wage ranks 50th of 51 jurisdictions, ahead of only Mississippi.
  • The monthly cost of a Florida home with a mortgage rose $313 after inflation from 2019 to 2024, $67 of it insurance.
  • Net domestic migration peaked at 310,892 in 2022 and fell to 22,517 in 2025, 93% below the peak.
