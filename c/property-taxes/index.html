Property Taxes

What would Amendment 3 change, for whom, and at what cost to local budgets?

Who decides
The Legislature placed Amendment 3 on the ballot on June 2, 2026, by 75-26 in the House and 30-9 in the Senate. Voters decide on November 3, 2026, and it needs 60% to pass.
What these charts establish
What the amendment's text does and who it reaches: homesteads, non-homestead property through a lower assessment cap, and renters not at all. It also carries the state Revenue Estimating Conference's estimate of lost local revenue, statewide and county by county. It does not show how counties and cities would respond.
  • By 2028 the maximum non-school homestead exemption would be $250,000 under Amendment 3, against $52,805 under current law.
  • From 2028, the exemption would cut a homestead's non-school property tax by at most $2,075 a year.
  • Florida's 2.92 million renter households would get no homestead exemption.
  • Amendment 3 would reduce local non-school property tax revenue by an estimated $11.83 billion a year by fiscal 2031-32.
  • Amendment 3 lists seven permitted uses for county and city property taxes.
  • Amendment 3 is on the ballot because the Legislature put it there: 75-26 in the House, 30-9 in the Senate.
  • As a share of today's budget, eight counties will lose more than half of their non-school property tax by 2032.
  • Ten counties carry $7.54 billion of the $11.83 billion, and 65% of the statewide total comes off county government.
  • Separately, the yearly assessment cap on rental and commercial property would fall from 10% to 5%.
